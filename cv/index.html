abstract = {Provenance research investigates the ownership histories of museum objects, but records are typically documented as semi-structured or unstructured free text, which limits computational and comparative analysis within and across institutions. This paper presents a reusable LLM-in-the-loop workflow—with all scripts and prompts shared—that transforms such records into structured, authority-linked data through schema-constrained extraction, source-grounding validation, entity pooling, Wikidata reconciliation, and disambiguation, with audit metadata preserved throughout to support traceability and human review. We applied the workflow to 1,829 provenance records of pre-1912 Chinese artworks at the Art Institute of Chicago. The workflow identified 314 distinct collecting actors, of which 114 were matched to Wikidata. Newly created Wikidata items were also incorporated in subsequent runs, showing that the workflow supports the participation of Libraries, Archives, and Museums (LAMs) in building shared authority data. Beyond the pilot dataset, the workflow contributes to digital scholarship infrastructure for non-Western collections, enabling researchers to map historical collecting networks and formulate new questions made possible by linked data.},
